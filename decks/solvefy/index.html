The reframe

The problem
was never the logo

Restated: customers did not experience five brands. They experienced five products that refused to behave like one. Fixing the surface without fixing that would have been a repaint.

Four moves

13
Move I

One brand, one promise

  • Five identities collapsed into one system
  • A single mark, and one line the whole company could say out loud
  • A visual language that survives a billboard and a button
  • Shipped with sales playbooks and two live events, so the commercial team told the same story the product did
14
Move I, continued

One mark,
ten signatures

  • Each product keeps its own name and its own colour
  • Every one is built the same way: the parent mark, a slash, the product
  • A customer who learns one learns all of them
  • The hub is where it pays off: every product in one place, each recognisable on its own and obviously the same company
15

The launch

16 May. One brand and one story, said out loud to the whole company and to the market on the same day.

  • Two live events, not a deck emailed round
  • The sales playbooks shipped with the brand system, so the commercial team told the same story the product did
16
Move II

One system underneath,
ten brands on top

  • Components read semantic tokens, never a brand name
  • One class swaps the whole product, so a new brand is configuration, not a rebuild
  • A colour now has a role instead of a hex, and contrast is checked at the token

Move III

Flows rebuilt around the job

Audience→ Channel→ Template→ Approval→ Schedule→ Billing→ Send
  • One screen per team that owned a field
  • Approval and billing sat in the path, and neither belonged to the person doing the work
  • Nothing saved, so an interrupted campaign started over
Who and what→ Message→ Review→ Send
  • One screen per decision the customer actually makes
  • Approval and billing moved into Review, off the critical path
  • Progress saves, and help sits at the two points people stopped
18
Move IV

The step by step

Dense B2B screens where the density is the point. Every surface runs on the shared system: same spacing scale, same states, same word for the same action.

Copy rules shipped with the components. Imperative for actions, concrete over abstract, sentence case everywhere.

What did not work

01I let the rebrand set the pace for too longTwo months of product decisions waited on a brand sign off they did not actually need
02The first token layer was too literalNamed after brands instead of roles, so the second sub brand broke it and we rewrote the semantic layer
03White label slipped to later, and stayed thereIt was the loudest ask in the interviews and the one I could not get prioritised against revenue work
27
Learnings

What I
took with me

The version of me that started this project would have delivered the rebrand that was asked for. The version that finished it treats the brief as a hypothesis.

01Interview before you agree to the briefThe reframe was the highest leverage thing I did, and it took three weeks
02Name tokens after roles, never after brandsA system is only multi brand if nothing inside it knows the brand's name
03Ship the story with the softwarePlaybooks and events were not marketing overhead, they were how the ecosystem became true outside the product
